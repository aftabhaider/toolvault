Compress Image

Make photos and images smaller for email, websites and upload limits. Pick a quality level and, for large photos, a maximum size; you see exactly how much each file shrinks.

Drop images hereor
    Quality
    Balanced is hard to tell apart from the original on screen.
    Maximum size
    Limits the longest side. Phone photos are often 4000 px or more; 1920 px is plenty for screens and email.
    Save as
    WebP is usually the smallest and works in all modern browsers; PNG files only get smaller when resized or saved as WebP or JPG.

    Private: files are processed on your device and never uploaded.

    How to use Compress Image

    1. Choose or drop your images.
    2. Pick a quality level and, for large photos, a maximum size.
    3. Select Compress images. Each file shows its size before and after; several files download as a ZIP.

    How image compression works

    JPG and WebP store photos with adjustable quality: a lower setting removes detail the eye barely notices. Most of the saving on phone photos comes from reducing their pixel dimensions, which is what the maximum-size option does.

    Files that are already small

    If an image would not get smaller at the chosen settings, it is skipped and listed as kept, so you never download a larger file.

    Frequently asked questions

    How do I reduce the file size of a photo?

    Choose the photo, keep Balanced quality and a maximum size of 1920 px, and select Compress images. A 4 MB phone photo usually becomes a few hundred kilobytes.

    Are my images uploaded?

    No. Compression runs in your browser using its built-in image encoder; the images never leave your device.

    Will compression remove location data?

    Yes. The compressed files are newly encoded and do not include the original’s embedded metadata, such as GPS location.

    Why did my PNG not get smaller?

    PNG is lossless, so its quality cannot be lowered. Choose a maximum size, or save as WebP or JPG for a much smaller file.
