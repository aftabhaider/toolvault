# ToolVault Calculator Content & SEO Standard

Every important calculator page should be a complete answer to the user's task, not just an input form.

## Required page structure

1. Calculator
2. How to use the calculator
3. What the calculator calculates
4. Formula(s) with variable definitions
5. Worked example using realistic values
6. Explanation of important inputs and units
7. Explanation of results
8. Accuracy, assumptions and limitations
9. Frequently Asked Questions
10. Related ToolVault calculators with contextual internal links

## Keyword coverage

For each calculator, identify:
- one primary keyword matching the main search intent;
- secondary variations and close semantic terms;
- long-tail questions users may ask;
- important entities, units, formulas and concepts.

Use these naturally in titles, headings, descriptions, paragraphs, examples, FAQ answers and internal-link anchor text. Do not repeat keywords unnaturally.

## Content principles

- The calculator remains the main purpose of the page.
- Content must explain the actual implementation, not a generic calculator description.
- Examples must use formulas and assumptions that match the live calculation engine.
- Do not invent industry standards, universal densities, coverage rates or limits.
- State assumptions explicitly and make adjustable assumptions editable where practical.
- Explain what the result does and does not mean.
- Avoid filler writing and arbitrary word-count targets.
- Do not create separate supporting articles unless the topic has meaningful additional search intent; keep small topics self-contained.

## Internal linking

Every calculator should link to:
- its parent category;
- at least two closely related calculators when available;
- a broader calculator/category collection.

Use canonical clean URLs without unnecessary index.html paths.

## Technical SEO checklist

- Unique title and meta description.
- One clear H1.
- Canonical URL.
- Crawlable HTML links.
- Appropriate WebApplication/WebPage/BreadcrumbList structured data when applicable.
- Sitemap entry.
- Mobile-friendly controls and readable units.
- No accidental noindex or blocked resources.
- No duplicate or misleading canonical.
- Core calculation must not depend on SEO copy.

## Accuracy checklist

Before publishing:
- Derive the formula independently.
- Verify unit conversions.
- Test a hand-checkable example.
- Test a boundary/error condition.
- Test inverse or identity relationships where applicable.
- Keep full precision internally and round display values only.
- Re-check labels against the actual units used by the code.

## AEO / answer readiness

Pages should make the answer easy to extract:
- question-style headings;
- short direct explanations;
- visible formulas;
- explicit variable meanings;
- worked numerical examples;
- clear assumptions;
- unambiguous output units.

Do not use a fake “AI SEO” layer or hidden keyword text. Strong people-first content and technically correct pages are the foundation.

## Content quality gate

A calculator is not considered complete until the tool, mathematics, explanation, examples, SEO metadata, internal links and sitemap all agree with one another.
